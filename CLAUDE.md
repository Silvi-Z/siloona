# CLAUDE.md — SILOONA
## Product Vision, Design System & Development Guidelines

---

# 1. PRODUCT VISION

Siloona is a premium digital invitation brand and platform focused on creating beautiful, personalized, interactive invitations for weddings and other meaningful life events.

Siloona is not intended to be a generic invitation template website.

The ultimate goal is to create a platform where every invitation feels like a carefully designed digital experience — personal, emotional, elegant, memorable, and visually distinctive.

The product should combine:
- High-end visual design.
- Personalization.
- Emotional storytelling.
- Smooth interactive experiences.
- Simple and intuitive user journeys.
- Reliable technical functionality.
- A premium brand identity.

Siloona should make customers feel that their event deserves something more special than a standard printed card or a generic online template.

## Core brand philosophy

"Every meaningful moment deserves its own story."

The invitation is not just a way to communicate event details. It is the first emotional impression of the event itself.

Siloona should transform that first impression into a memorable experience.

---

# 2. BRAND POSITIONING

## Market

Primary market: Armenia.

The product should initially focus on Armenian customers and Armenian-language invitations.

The long-term product should be capable of supporting multiple languages and markets.

## Target audience

Primary audience:
- Couples preparing for weddings.
- People organizing engagement ceremonies.
- Families organizing birthdays and celebrations.
- People planning baby celebrations, gender reveal parties, christenings, and other meaningful events.

The visual and UX direction should appeal to customers who value aesthetics, individuality, and quality.

## Brand personality

Siloona should feel:
- Elegant.
- Modern.
- Emotional.
- Artistic.
- Personal.
- Refined.
- Confident.
- Premium, but not inaccessible.

Avoid making the brand feel:
- Cheap.
- Generic.
- Overly commercial.
- Childishly decorative.
- Visually cluttered.
- Like a mass-produced template marketplace.

---

# 3. THE FINAL PRODUCT

The long-term vision is to create a complete digital invitation platform.

The platform should have two main experiences:

1. The Siloona website — where customers discover, explore, and order invitations.
2. The digital invitations themselves — personalized interactive websites created for individual events.

These two experiences must have distinct design systems and purposes.

The main website represents the Siloona brand.

Individual invitations represent the customer's event and personal story.

Do not make all invitations look like variations of the main website.

---

# 4. SILOONA MAIN WEBSITE

The website should act as a premium digital showroom and customer acquisition platform.

Its primary purpose is to:
- Present the brand.
- Demonstrate the quality of invitations.
- Help customers discover suitable designs.
- Explain the customization process.
- Build trust.
- Convert visitors into paying customers.

## Main website structure

The following structure represents the intended product direction. Preserve the existing implementation where appropriate and develop missing sections progressively.

### Homepage

The homepage should immediately communicate the aesthetic and value of Siloona.

It should include:

- A visually strong hero section.
- A concise brand message.
- Selected invitation designs.
- Event categories.
- A showcase of distinctive invitation experiences.
- An explanation of the ordering process.
- A section highlighting personalization.
- A clear call to action.
- Contact and social media information.

The homepage should not feel like a conventional SaaS landing page.

Avoid excessive cards, repetitive sections, unnecessary icons, and generic marketing layouts.

### Invitation gallery

Customers should be able to explore different invitation designs.

The gallery should eventually support:
- Wedding invitations.
- Birthday invitations.
- Engagement invitations.
- Baby celebrations.
- Gender reveal invitations.
- Other event categories.

Each design should have its own visual identity.

The gallery should prioritize visual presentation over excessive text.

### Invitation detail pages

Each design should have a dedicated presentation page containing:
- A preview of the invitation.
- A description of its concept.
- A demonstration of its interactive elements.
- Information about customization possibilities.
- Pricing, when established.
- A clear order CTA.

The preview should accurately represent the actual invitation.

### Ordering process

The ordering process should be simple and understandable.

The intended customer journey:

1. Discover Siloona.
2. Explore invitation designs.
3. Open a selected design.
4. View its full interactive demo.
5. Decide to order.
6. Submit event information and personalization requirements.
7. Communicate with Siloona if necessary.
8. Receive a personalized invitation.
9. Share the invitation with guests.

Do not introduce unnecessary steps.

### About Siloona

The brand presentation should communicate the philosophy behind personalized digital invitations.

Avoid invented testimonials, fake reviews, fictional achievements, or unsupported claims.

### Contact

Provide accessible contact options, especially Instagram and Telegram where appropriate.

---

# 5. DIGITAL INVITATION EXPERIENCE

This is the core product of Siloona.

Every invitation should feel like a small, beautifully art-directed website.

The invitation must be:
- Visually memorable.
- Fully responsive.
- Easy to navigate.
- Fast to load.
- Emotionally engaging.
- Simple for guests to use.

## Invitation design philosophy

Each invitation should be designed around a specific visual concept.

Examples of possible design directions:
- Minimalist editorial.
- Romantic cinematic.
- Modern luxury.
- Contemporary typography.
- Soft elegant.
- Artistic and expressive.
- Traditional elements interpreted in a modern way.

These are creative directions, not mandatory templates.

Do not force every invitation into the same layout.

## Wedding invitation functionality

Depending on the selected design, wedding invitations may include:

- Couple names.
- Event date.
- Countdown timer.
- Hero section.
- Couple photographs.
- Love story.
- Event schedule.
- Bride's house location.
- Groom's house location.
- Ceremony location.
- Reception location.
- Interactive map links.
- RSVP form.
- Dress code.
- Photo gallery.
- Music or sound elements where appropriate.
- Personalized messages.
- Animated transitions.
- Scroll-based effects.

Not every invitation needs every feature.

The design should determine which features are appropriate.

## Personalization

The ultimate product should support meaningful personalization.

Customers should be able to personalize relevant content such as:
- Names.
- Event date and time.
- Photographs.
- Text.
- Locations.
- Event schedule.
- Dress code.
- Color accents.
- Other design-specific elements.

Personalization should preserve the artistic integrity of the invitation.

Do not allow customization to make the design visually inconsistent.

## RSVP

RSVP functionality should be reliable and easy to use.

The intended system should support:
- Guest attendance confirmation.
- Guest name.
- Attendance status.
- Additional guests where applicable.
- Optional custom questions.
- Submission feedback.

RSVP responses should be stored reliably and made accessible to the event organizers.

The long-term goal is to provide an organized RSVP management experience.

## Maps and event locations

Location information should be clear and useful.

Each location may include:
- Venue name.
- Address.
- Map preview.
- A button to open directions.

Do not make guests search manually for event locations.

## Animations

Animations should enhance the invitation experience.

Use:
- Subtle transitions.
- Smooth scroll effects.
- Elegant reveals.
- Carefully timed visual movement.
- Interactive details.

Avoid:
- Excessive animation.
- Distracting motion.
- Heavy effects that slow down the page.
- Animations that interfere with usability.
- Unnecessary motion on mobile devices.

Motion should feel intentional, not decorative for its own sake.

---

# 6. DESIGN SYSTEM

## Overall visual direction

Siloona should maintain a strong design identity without making every page visually identical.

The brand website should use a refined, modern, editorial aesthetic.

The invitation designs should have their own independent art direction.

## Typography

Typography is one of the most important aspects of Siloona.

Use typography as a primary design element.

Prioritize:
- Beautiful typography.
- Strong hierarchy.
- Thoughtful spacing.
- Appropriate contrast.
- Readability.
- Armenian language compatibility.

Armenian typography must be treated as a first-class design requirement.

Do not replace existing fonts without a clear reason.

Always verify Armenian characters visually.

Avoid fonts that render Armenian text poorly or inconsistently.

## Color

The main brand may use restrained colors and carefully selected accents.

Individual invitations may use entirely different palettes.

Avoid applying the same brand color palette to every invitation.

## Layout

Prioritize:
- Composition.
- Whitespace.
- Visual balance.
- Editorial layouts.
- Strong imagery.
- Clear hierarchy.
- Intentional asymmetry where appropriate.

Avoid:
- Generic SaaS card grids.
- Excessively rounded containers.
- Repetitive layouts.
- Unnecessary shadows.
- Arbitrary gradients.
- Overly decorative interfaces.

## Imagery

Images should be an essential part of the visual experience.

Use high-quality, appropriate imagery.

Do not use random stock photos that contradict the design concept.

When placeholder images are necessary, make their replacement easy.

---

# 7. UX PRINCIPLES

The product must balance visual beauty with usability.

The following principles are mandatory:

1. Guests must understand the invitation without instructions.
2. Event information must be easy to find.
3. RSVP must be straightforward.
4. Important actions must be clearly identifiable.
5. The invitation must work well on mobile devices.
6. Animations must never obstruct important information.
7. The experience must remain elegant and functional.
8. Forms must provide clear validation and feedback.
9. Navigation must be intuitive.
10. The design must not sacrifice usability for visual effects.

The majority of invitation guests are expected to open the invitation on their phones.

Mobile-first quality is therefore essential.

---

# 8. TECHNICAL PRODUCT DIRECTION

The project may evolve into a more structured platform over time.

Potential long-term capabilities include:

- A reusable invitation component system.
- Structured invitation content.
- Design-specific configuration.
- Personalized invitation generation.
- Customer order management.
- RSVP management.
- Invitation publishing.
- Unique invitation URLs.
- Customer-specific invitation pages.
- Data management.
- Administrative tools.

These are long-term product goals.

Do not assume that every feature already exists.

Do not implement major architectural changes without evaluating the current codebase first.

## Architecture principles

The codebase should prioritize:
- Maintainability.
- Reusability where appropriate.
- Clear separation of concerns.
- Reliable functionality.
- Responsive behavior.
- Performance.
- Simplicity.

Do not introduce unnecessary abstractions.

Do not create a complex platform architecture before it is justified by actual product requirements.

## Existing invitation demos

The project currently contains separate HTML invitation demos and a main Siloona website.

These should be treated as valuable existing assets.

Do not automatically convert all invitations into a shared template.

Each invitation may have a unique visual structure and interactions.

Shared functionality may be extracted when it is genuinely useful.

---

# 9. BUSINESS MODEL

Siloona is intended to be a paid digital invitation service.

The primary initial revenue source is personalized invitation orders.

The product should emphasize:
- Design quality.
- Personalization.
- Customer experience.
- Reliable delivery.
- Perceived value.

Avoid competing primarily on the lowest price.

The long-term business may expand into additional event types and more scalable ordering processes.

Do not invent pricing, discounts, packages, or business policies.

Use only confirmed information.

# 10. DEVELOPMENT RULES FOR CLAUDE CODE

These instructions are mandatory.

## Before making changes

Always:

1. Inspect the existing project structure.
2. Understand the relevant files.
3. Identify the current implementation.
4. Determine what the task actually requires.
5. Preserve existing functionality unless explicitly instructed otherwise.

Do not assume that a new implementation is better than the existing one.

## Visual preservation

When the user provides a screenshot, reference design, HTML demo, or Figma design:

- Treat it as the primary visual reference.
- Reproduce the composition accurately.
- Preserve spacing, typography, proportions, and layout.
- Do not invent alternative designs.
- Do not simplify the reference without permission.
- Do not add unnecessary visual elements.

The task is to implement the reference, not reinterpret it.

## Scope control

Do not:
- Modify unrelated pages.
- Refactor unrelated components.
- Change global styles unnecessarily.
- Replace fonts without approval.
- Change existing text without approval.
- Remove existing functionality.
- Add features that were not requested.
- Introduce dependencies without justification.

If a requested change requires modifications outside the stated scope, explain why before proceeding when the impact is significant.

## Design quality

Do not settle for a generic or mediocre visual result.

When creating new designs:
- Establish a clear concept.
- Use intentional composition.
- Pay attention to typography.
- Maintain consistent spacing.
- Ensure responsive quality.
- Avoid generic AI-generated design patterns.

However, do not redesign an already approved interface merely because another design might be more attractive.

## Code quality

Write clean, understandable, maintainable code.

Avoid:
- Unnecessary complexity.
- Duplicate logic.
- Excessive inline styles where a suitable styling system exists.
- Unnecessary dependencies.
- Unrelated refactoring.

Follow the existing project conventions.

## Testing

After implementation:
- Verify the affected page.
- Check desktop behavior.
- Check mobile behavior.
- Verify important interactions.
- Check forms and navigation.
- Check for console errors where possible.
- Ensure that existing functionality remains intact.

Do not claim that something was tested if it was not.

## Communication

When completing a task, provide a concise summary:
- What changed.
- Which files were affected.
- What was tested.
- Any remaining limitations.

Do not provide lengthy explanations of obvious implementation details.

# 11. PRODUCT PRIORITIES

The product should evolve in a deliberate order.

## Phase 1 — Brand and sales readiness

Primary goal: acquire the first paying customers.

Priorities:
- Complete the main website.
- Present invitation demos professionally.
- Make the ordering process clear.
- Ensure contact options work.
- Make mobile experiences excellent.
- Establish a consistent brand identity.

## Phase 2 — Reliable invitation production

Priorities:
- Improve the invitation creation workflow.
- Organize personalization data.
- Standardize delivery.
- Make RSVP handling reliable.
- Reduce repetitive manual work.

## Phase 3 — Platform development

Priorities:
- Reusable invitation infrastructure.
- Better order management.
- RSVP dashboards.
- Structured customer data.
- Unique invitation publishing.
- More efficient personalization.

## Phase 4 — Scalability

Potential future direction:
- More event categories.
- More invitation designs.
- Multiple languages.
- Advanced personalization.
- Customer self-service.
- More automated workflows.

Do not prioritize scalability over the ability to deliver excellent invitations and acquire customers today.

# 12. DEFINITION OF A SUCCESSFUL SILOONA PRODUCT

The final product should satisfy the following criteria:

### Brand
- Siloona has a recognizable visual identity.
- The website feels premium and intentional.
- The brand communicates emotional value.

### Invitations
- Each invitation feels unique.
- The design is polished.
- The experience is interactive where appropriate.
- Personalization feels natural.
- Armenian text is rendered beautifully.

### Customer experience
- Customers can discover designs easily.
- Ordering is understandable.
- Guests can navigate invitations intuitively.
- RSVP works reliably.

### Technical quality
- The website is responsive.
- Pages load efficiently.
- The code is maintainable.
- Existing functionality is preserved.
- Errors are handled properly.

### Business
- The product is commercially usable.
- Customers understand what they are purchasing.
- The service can deliver real value.
- The product can evolve without unnecessary complexity.

---

# 13. FINAL GUIDING PRINCIPLE

Siloona should not become just another website selling digital invitation templates.

It should become a distinctive digital invitation brand known for creating personalized, elegant, emotionally meaningful experiences.

Every design decision, technical implementation, and product feature should contribute to this vision.

The ultimate standard is:

**Beautiful enough to make people fall in love with the invitation. Functional enough to trust it with an important event. Personal enough to make it feel uniquely theirs.**

When making decisions, prioritize:

1. Product quality.
2. Visual excellence.
3. Customer experience.
4. Reliability.
5. Business practicality.
6. Maintainability.

Do not sacrifice the core product vision for unnecessary complexity or superficial visual trends.
